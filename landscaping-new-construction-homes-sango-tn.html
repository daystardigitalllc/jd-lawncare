Landscaping Tips for New Construction Homes in Sango, TN

Home • Blog • New Construction
New Construction

Sango is one of the fastest-growing pockets of Montgomery County, and new subdivisions keep going up along Rossview Road and Highway 76 every year. If you just closed on a new-construction home here, your yard almost certainly needs more attention than the builder's sod job suggests. Here's what to prioritize.

1. Check the Grading Before You Plant Anything

Construction equipment compacts soil and often leaves inconsistent grading behind. Before investing in landscaping or new plant beds, it's worth confirming water is draining away from the foundation correctly — regrading now is far easier than fixing it after beds and hardscaping are in.

2. Give New Sod Real Soil to Root Into

Builder-grade sod is often laid directly over compacted, low-quality fill soil. If your lawn is struggling to establish despite watering, the soil underneath — not the grass itself — is usually the problem. Aerating and topdressing with quality soil can make a significant difference in the first year.

3. Plan Garden Beds Around Mature Sizing

It's tempting to landscape a new-build yard exactly to scale with the current bare look, but shrubs and trees planted too close together outgrow their space within a few years. Planning bed layouts around mature plant sizing saves a full redo down the road.

4. Get Ahead of Erosion on Sloped Lots

Many Sango lots have some grade to them, and newly disturbed soil erodes fast before turf and root systems establish. Mulch, erosion control fabric, or terracing on steeper sections can prevent bare, washed-out patches from becoming a recurring problem.

5. Don't Rush Hardscaping Before the Ground Settles

Freshly disturbed and backfilled soil around a new home continues to settle for a period after construction. Patios, walkways, or retaining walls built too early on unsettled ground are more likely to shift — which is why DIY hardscaping on new lots often runs into trouble.
